# Creative Direction

## Goal
Give the product a point of view before any component is drawn. A written direction prevents drift between pages.

## Direction brief (write this first, 6 lines maximum)
- **Audience:** who, their context, their expectations.
- **Feeling:** three adjectives (e.g., precise, warm, confident).
- **Visual anchor:** one idea that organizes the look (e.g., "a paper ledger", "a lit stage", "a control room").
- **Color role:** the accent's job and how often it appears (aim for 5–10% of the surface).
- **Type voice:** one display style and one text style, with the reason.
- **Avoid:** two or three things this brand must never look like.

## Choosing an anchor
Good anchors come from the product itself: its workflow, its material, its physical or social setting. A studio booking product might use a "rehearsal call sheet" structure; a store might use "editorial product spreads". The anchor should shape layout, not just color.

## Reference use
Look at references for pattern and quality, not for copying. Extract principles (spacing, rhythm, contrast) and build your own expression.

## Asset direction
- Photography and illustration must share one treatment (lighting, color grade, line weight).
- Icons come from one set with one stroke width.
- Do not mix flat, glossy, and outlined styles.

## Review question
Could a stranger identify the brand from one screen with the logo hidden? If not, the direction is too weak.
